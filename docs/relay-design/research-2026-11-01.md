# zeUTXO phase 2: approaches to a private (shielded) ZEC <-> ADA atomic swap

Prepared Sunday Nov 1, 2026, ~11:30 PM ET, for John. This is web and paper research only. I did not touch GGLori and ran no builds or proofs. It builds on `/workspace/zeutxo-audit-2026-11-01.md`, which I refer to as "the audit".

**How to read this**
- Every outside fact has a link. I opened each source this session, either by fetching the page or through a search-result extract.
- Anything I could not verify is marked **[unverified]**.
- My own reasoning is marked **[analysis]**.
- Figures from your own project docs are cited by file name (from the audit).

**Your steer:** a fully trustless swap is the end goal. Relaxed options are studied as stepping stones. For each one I give what it teaches, how it can be upgraded to trustless, and what would have to get cheaper or more mature first. The ranking is by how well each path leads to a trustless end state, not by short-term ease.

## 0. Summary (read this first)

1. **What "trustless" can mean.** Every cross-chain swap in the literature, including every relay design in your own R2 settlement work, relies on at least one timing assumption. Usually it's that some honest party gets a transaction or a challenge mined within a known window. Your own research doc already states this ("No state machine can promise both finite refunds and safe already-sent payments under arbitrary censorship and outages", ALTERNATIVE-CONSTRUCTIONS.md). The best known open designs, Binocular for Bitcoin on Cardano and FlyClient, also assume one honest party and bounded delay ([Binocular whitepaper](https://github.com/lantr-io/binocular/blob/main/Whitepaper.md); R2-SETTLEMENT-PROTOCOL-REVIEW.md on FlyClient).

   **[analysis]** So the realistic definition of "fully trustless" is: *no party beyond the two chains' consensus, plus one honest watcher, with a numeric delay bound written down.* "Safe under unbounded delay" is not achievable by anyone. Accepting this definition is the single biggest unblocker, and it doesn't add trust in any person.

2. **Top recommendation (best route to trustless): an optimistic Zcash header relay on Cardano.**
   - **The pattern:** this follows Binocular's design. Anyone posts Zcash headers. Cardano checks the cheap rules. The expensive Equihash proof-of-work check runs only when someone disputes a header. A challenge window and a fork tree that picks the chain with the most work handle forks.
   - **Settlement:** ADA settles when Cardano sees a *known Zcash transaction ID* included in a confirmed block. Your R2 claim and refund transactions have fixed IDs in advance, because Zcash transaction IDs exclude signatures and proofs ([ZIP 244](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0244)).
   - **What this replaces:** it removes most of the 652-position STWO-in-Groth16 proof graph. ZK proving is needed only for disputes, and for upgrading the starting checkpoint into a proven one later.
   - **What it reuses:** R2's settlement state machine (Armed/Pending/outcome), M01–M07, and R1's Equihash circuit (26.65M constraints) as the dispute proof.

3. **Second: R2 with adaptor signatures instead of a shared hash, under a written risk limit.**
   - **What changes:** the ADA side is claimed by a Schnorr signature. Completing that signature mathematically reveals the secret Alice needs to complete her RedPallas signature on the Orchard spend. A cross-curve proof (DLEQ) ties the two curves together.
   - **Privacy:** this removes R2's public hash link between the two chains.
   - **What stays the same:** it is still the classic two-deadline swap, so it needs a written numeric risk limit.
   - **Why it's on the trustless path:** it is the user-facing protocol that the relay in #1 can later "arm", turning its deadlines into outcome-driven settlement.

4. **Third (long-run research track): a validity-proof Zcash light client.** Use Zoro, a STARK proof of Zcash headers built in Cairo/STWO ([forum](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/zoro-a-stark-proven-zcash-light-client-in-cairo/53220)), wrapped into one Groth16 proof (about 2.05–2.71B CPU units on Cardano; [ZeroJ](https://zeroj.dev/guides/verifying/on-chain/), [ODATANO](https://odatano.dev/docs/dayzero/)). This is what turns #1's checkpoint and disputes into pure proofs.

5. **Not recommended as main paths, but useful as stepping stones or benchmarks:**
   - **Committees and custodial networks:** NEAR Intents and Maya are custodial TSS vaults with transparent ZEC deposits.
   - **Zcash protocol features:** ZSAs are not in NU7.
   - **Bitcoin-research ideas:** Bitcoin PIPEs v2 needs about 330 TB per ciphertext today. The Shielded Bitcoin paper leaves peg-in and peg-out out of scope.

6. **Time-sensitive: NU7 activates around Nov 5, 2026** (height 3,543,405).
   - **Shorter blocks:** Zcash blocks go from 75 s to 25 s ([Zebra 7.0.0](https://zfnd.org/zebra-7-0-0-release/)). Every deadline counted in blocks, and every relay cost model, must be recalibrated.
   - **v4 transactions become invalid** ([ZIP 259](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0259)). The transparent MVP's v4 P2SH HTLC code stops working on mainnet and testnet after activation. R2's v5/v6 path is not affected by this rule.

## 2. The approaches

For each approach the notes cover: **what it is**, **which rule it relaxes**, **privacy kept**, **what R1/R2 work is reused**, **feasibility on your PC**, **tooling maturity**, **toward trustless** (what it teaches and its upgrade path), and **key sources**.

### (a) Bounded-risk HTLC using R2's transparent auxiliary input ("R2 as built")

- **What:** R2 exactly as built. A 2-of-2 FROST Orchard/Ironwood note plus a small transparent P2SH auxiliary output with a hash branch and a CLTV refund branch. A pre-signed shielded refund. The ADA side is a hash-lock contract. The only change is that you accept a **written, numeric risk limit** instead of demanding safety under unbounded delay.
- **Rule relaxed:** unbounded-delay safety. Nothing else: no new party, no checkpoint.
- **Privacy:** principal stays shielded. Not kept: the shared hash/secret links the two chains (R2-DESIGN.md), and the auxiliary output is visibly swap-shaped.
- **Reuse:** M01–M07 and M08 P2 nearly as-is (R2-COMPLETION-CHECKLIST.md). Needs NU7 recalibration (25 s blocks).
- **How to set a numeric threshold [analysis, using the data in §1]:**
  - Define **D** = the longest delay you're willing to assume for a fee-paying transaction to be mined and settled, on either chain. The swap is safe if every honest step lands within D.
  - Deadlines: Alice's ADA refund deadline T_A sits at least D_Cardano_settle + D_Zcash_include + an offline allowance before Bob's ZEC refund height T_B. Bob's ZEC claim must be watched and answered within D.
  - Starting data points:
    - Cardano practical settlement is a few minutes; the hard bound is 12 h.
    - Zcash exchange confirmation practice is 100–110 blocks. That was ~2.5 h at 75 s blocks; at 25 s blocks the same block count is ~46 min. **[analysis]** This is arithmetic only; whether exchanges will keep counting blocks or switch to counting time after NU7 is **[unverified]**.
  - Present the limit to you as a sentence, e.g.: "You lose only if a correctly-fee'd transaction is not mined for more than D = 6 h while you are online." D is your choice; 6 h is just an example.
  - Measure on testnet (or historically on mainnet) how often inclusion takes longer than D at the fee rule in [ZIP 317](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0317) **[link not fetched this session]**. This is exactly the "residual-risk criterion" the M10 decision said was missing (R2-M10-VIABILITY-DECISION.md).
- **Your PC:** easy. No proving at all.
- **Maturity:** HTLCs on Zcash's transparent pool are standard ([Logos RFP appendix](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/logos-co/rfp/master/appendix/zcash-atomic-swap-primitives.md)). FROST for Orchard is a draft ZIP with an implementation ([ZIP 312](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0312)).
- **Toward trustless:**
  - *What it teaches:* the real delay distributions; fee bumping (M09); two independent hosts (M08 P3).
  - *Upgrade path:* keep the protocol, but replace "ADA refunds after T_A" with "ADA follows the proven Zcash outcome" once relay (d) exists. That is R2-SETTLEMENT-DESIGN.md's Armed mode.
  - *Needs:* the relay in (d).
- **Sources:** above, plus the Zwap ECDH variant ([forum](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/zwap-unlinkable-cross-chain-atomic-swaps/55104)).

### (b) Adaptor-signature ("scriptless") swap with a shielded Orchard leg

- **What:** same skeleton as (a), but there is no shared hash.
  - ADA is locked in a Cardano script that pays Bob when he provides a valid **BIP-340 Schnorr secp256k1 signature** under a jointly derived key. Plutus can check that directly ([CIP-49](https://cips.cardano.org/cip/CIP-49)).
  - Bob's signature completes an adaptor pre-signature, and that reveals a secret scalar *t*.
  - A **cross-curve DLEQ** proof made up front shows that the same *t* sits behind a Pallas point. So Alice can use *t* to complete her **RedPallas** pre-signature on the jointly owned Orchard note.
  - The completed Orchard spend signature is *public in the transaction data*: ZIP 244 commits `vSpendAuthSigsOrchard` and `rk` into the block ([ZIP 244](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0244)). So the counterparty can extract what they need, while outsiders see an ordinary shielded spend.
  - **[analysis]** A forum commenter's claim that "the other party can't even see the broadcasted transaction" doesn't match the transaction format: the signatures are public even though the amounts aren't.
- **Hard parts, per sources:**
  - Orchard spend keys are re-randomized for every spend. Pre-signer and completer must agree on the randomizer α in advance. This is "not a fundamental obstacle", but "no published paper or code specifically constructs an adaptor pre-signature against Sapling or Orchard spend authorization" ([Logos RFP appendix](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/logos-co/rfp/master/appendix/zcash-atomic-swap-primitives.md)).
  - Cross-curve DLEQ precedent exists for ed25519↔secp256k1 ([Farcaster dleq.rs](https://docs.rs/farcaster_core/latest/src/farcaster_core/crypto/dleq.rs.html), [COMIT](https://comit.network/blog/2020/10/06/monero-bitcoin/)).
  - Pallas↔secp256k1 DLEQ code is claimed by Zwap and appears in an open wallet PR ([Zwap early access](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/zwap-trustless-shielded-atomic-swaps-for-zcash-early-access/55874), [vizor-wallet PR](https://github.com/chainapsis/vizor-wallet/pull/323) **[PR not opened this session]**).
  - On Hanh's 2023 design, one reply says the DLEQ issues "are resolved by not using Halo 2 but an arithmetic proof" ([Zwap thread](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/zwap-unlinkable-cross-chain-atomic-swaps/55104)).
- **Refund asymmetry:** with a pure joint shielded key, the ZEC holder can't refund alone ([ASMR critique in the Zwap thread](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/zwap-unlinkable-cross-chain-atomic-swaps/55104)). **[analysis]** R2's transparent auxiliary input plus the pre-signed refund is exactly what fixes that, which is why combining (a)'s refund structure with (b)'s claim path is the right variant.
- **Rule relaxed:** same as (a), bounded delay. It also needs a written risk limit; adaptor signatures don't fix the timing problem ("no cure", COMIT A3.4, cited in R2-SAFETY-SOLUTION-RESEARCH.md).
- **Privacy:** better than (a). No deterministic link between the chains; what remains is timing and amount correlation, plus the visible auxiliary output and the visible Cardano script.
- **Reuse:** R2's FROST/DKG, auxiliary refund, journals and recovery. The ADA validator changes from a hash check to a signature check.
- **Your PC:** easy. The DLEQ is a small proof.
- **Maturity:** research grade for Orchard.
  - Zwap claimed Orchard-native trustless swaps (ETH/USDC) in May 2026, capped at $1–$100, with two internal security reviews and no external audit.
  - Later posts in Aug 2026 report the site not opening and question how the whitepaper matches Orchard's lack of scripts. Status: **unclear / possibly inactive** ([thread](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/zwap-trustless-shielded-atomic-swaps-for-zcash-early-access/55874)).
  - Universal Atomic Swaps gives a general framework ([eprint 2021/1612](https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/1612)).
- **Toward trustless:**
  - *What it teaches:* α-aware RedPallas adaptor signatures and cross-curve DLEQ. Both are needed for any fully shielded design.
  - *Upgrade path:* same as (a), via the relay.
  - *Needs:* an external review of the RedPallas adaptor construction.

### (c) Zcash protocol features and existing ZEC cross-chain products

- **ZSAs:** not in NU7, no activation date, and not a programmable contract layer ([ZecHub](https://zechub.wiki/zcash-tech/zcash-shielded-assets)). They don't help a ZEC↔ADA swap directly. **Verdict:** not a path.
- **STARK_VERIFY TZE / Ztarknet:** a draft ZIP for a Circle-STARK verifier on Zcash, open PR last updated Mar 24, 2026 ([zips PR 1107](https://github.com/zcash/zips/pull/1107)); Ztarknet is proof-of-concept ([Ztarknet repo](https://github.com/Ztarknet/ztarknet)). **[analysis]** If it ever activated, Zcash could verify proofs *about Cardano*, which would allow symmetric designs. That would be a long-term consensus change; not a path now.
- **Crosslink:** a BFT finality layer for Zcash, in productionization ([roadmap](https://shieldedlabs.net/roadmap/)). **[analysis]** Finality certificates signed by Zcash's *own* consensus finalizers would be a much cheaper thing for Cardano to verify than proof-of-work. They would also remove the "withheld heavier fork" problem from R2-SETTLEMENT-DESIGN.md. This is an important future upgrade for (d) and (g). The signature scheme and finalizer-set details are **[unverified]**.
- **NEAR Intents / Zashi (now Zodl):**
  - Shielded ZEC swaps live since Nov 2025 ([Zcash forum](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/shielded-swaps-in-zashi/53204)).
  - NEAR's docs say ZEC is "Partially supported – Transparent addresses only" and Cardano is "Partially supported" ([NEAR chain support](https://docs.near-intents.org/resources/chain-support)).
  - Funds sit in validators' TSS vaults; a community note says true Orchard support would need FROST rather than TSS ([NEAR Intents thread](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/near-intents/54588)).
  - **Trust:** an MPC committee holds the coins (custodial).
- **Maya Protocol:**
  - Shielded ZEC *outbound* destinations (Orchard preferred), but inbound deposits go to a transparent vault; outputs use an all-zero outgoing viewing key, so amount and memo are publicly recoverable ([Maya MR 625](https://gitlab.com/mayachain/mayanode/-/merge_requests/625), [grant thread](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/maya-protocol-advanced-shielded-zec-support-retroactive-grant/54593/15)).
  - Supports ADA ([Maya docs](https://docs.mayaprotocol.com/introduction/maya-ecosystem/user-interfaces-and-wallets)).
  - **[analysis]** So ZEC↔ADA via Maya exists today: custodial TSS vault, partly shielded, not atomic.
- **THORChain:** native ZEC since Oct 2026, transparent only, briefly halted by a bug ([THORChain blog](https://blog.thorchain.org/bringing-native-zcash-to-thorchain), [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/thorchain-halts-zec-swaps-rune-bug/)).
- **Toward trustless:** these are benchmarks, not stepping stones. Their lesson is that users accept committee custody for convenience. The upgrade path would be TSS → FROST for Orchard, still a committee. They don't lead to a trustless end state.

### (d) Optimistic / bonded header relays and BitVM-style verification on Cardano

- **What:** **Binocular** is a permissionless Bitcoin oracle on Cardano ([whitepaper](https://github.com/lantr-io/binocular/blob/main/Whitepaper.md)). It is the closest working template.
  - Anyone submits headers. Plutus checks proof-of-work, difficulty and timestamps.
  - Competing forks live in a fork tree; the chain with the most work wins.
  - A block is promoted to "confirmed" only after 100+ confirmations **and** 200 minutes on Cardano. That window lets honest parties counter a fork the attacker mined in private.
  - Transaction inclusion is checked against the confirmed set with Merkle proofs.
  - It starts from a **checkpoint** and assumes one honest party.
  - Its owner can reset the state only after 30 days without updates.
- **Zcash differences [analysis]:**
  1. Checking a Zcash header's proof-of-work means checking an **Equihash(200,9)** solution. R1 measured a direct on-chain check at 19.28B CPU, over budget (DESTINATION-CHECKPOINT.md).
  2. So the relay would check the cheap rules on-chain. Assuming, **[to verify in step 1]**, that the Zcash block hash is double-SHA256 like Bitcoin, the hash-below-target test is cheap. **[unverified]**
  3. The Equihash solution itself would be accepted *optimistically*: the submitter posts a bond, and anyone can challenge a specific header within the window. The challenger, or the defender, proves the solution's validity or invalidity with a **single Groth16 proof** (~2–2.7B CPU on Cardano, §1). R1's Equihash circuit (26,650,078 constraints, DESTINATION-CHECKPOINT.md) or its staged version (4 × 8,212,125 + 7,152,467) is the starting point.
  4. After NU7 there are ~3,456 headers a day (86,400 s / 25 s). Batching per transaction, Binocular-style, is required.
  5. Settlement: ADA's Armed state releases when a Merkle proof shows that the pre-known **claim txid** or **refund txid** is in a confirmed block. Zcash v5+ txids are fixed in advance and `hashMerkleRoot` is built over them ([ZIP 244](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0244)). No proof verification is needed on the normal path.
- **BitVM-style on Cardano:** Cardinal (BitVMX, 1-of-n honest operators) did a mainnet round trip but is "not production-ready" ([cardinal-spec](https://github.com/input-output-hk/cardinal-spec), [FLUID blog](https://pool.fluid7.com/blog/bitcoin-defi-on-cardano)). BitVM-style bisection was built for Bitcoin, which has almost no scripting. Cardano can verify Groth16 directly, so a single-proof dispute is simpler than bisection.
- **Rule relaxed:** start from a **trusted checkpoint** instead of genesis; "one honest watcher plus a window W" (R2 already accepted this); bonds.
- **Privacy:** Cardano sees which Zcash txid settled the swap, so the auxiliary/claim transaction is linked to the ADA escrow on-chain. The note contents stay shielded. **[analysis]** This is about the same linkage as R2's hash.
- **Reuse:** R2's settlement core and finite-state model (R2-SETTLEMENT-DESIGN.md), plus M01–M07; R1's Equihash circuits; the Lucid emulator. This drops the 652-position decomposition (R2-PROOF-FEASIBILITY.md) entirely.
- **Your PC:** the normal path needs no proving. The dispute proof needs an Equihash Groth16 setup. R1's leaf setup was 1,355 s at 8.2M constraints under a cap (DESTINATION-CHECKPOINT.md). Whether the full 26.65M-constraint circuit fits in ~63 GiB of RAM is **[unverified]**; measure it.
- **Maturity:** Binocular is a draft v0.4 with a code repository. Nothing like it exists for Zcash.
- **Toward trustless — what it teaches:** real relay costs, watcher operations, and fork handling.
- **Upgrade path:**
  1. Trusted checkpoint → *proven* checkpoint: one Zoro/STWO receipt from genesis to the checkpoint, checked off-chain by anyone or wrapped in Groth16 once. See (g).
  2. Optimistic Equihash → validity proofs per batch, once proving gets cheap.
  3. Proof-of-work fork choice → Crosslink finality certificates, if Crosslink activates.

  After step 1, the only assumptions left are Zcash's honest-majority proof-of-work and one honest watcher within W. That is the realistic definition of "fully trustless" from §0.
- **Needs:** a Zoro receipt for the history, or a cheaper Equihash proof.

### (e) Committees, federations, threshold signers, and trusted checkpoints

- **What:** a k-of-n set of attesters signs the statement "Zcash txid X is final at height h", and Cardano checks their signatures.
  - **Tooling:** Mithril is stake-weighted threshold certificates *about Cardano*, which suits Cardano → Zcash-side clients rather than proving Zcash to Cardano ([Mithril docs](https://mithril.network/doc/)). Partner Chains toolkit (committee selection, multisig governance) is alpha ([repo](https://github.com/midnightntwrk/partner-chains)).
  - **Precedents:** Midnight's Cardano bridge needs a governance-set initial checkpoint and has ~12 h finality ([C-to-M bridge enabling](https://github.com/midnightntwrk/midnight-node/blob/main/docs/c-to-m-bridge-enabling.md), [MIP-20](https://github.com/midnightntwrk/midnight-improvement-proposals/issues/20)). Cardinal uses 1-of-n operators ([cardinal-spec](https://github.com/input-output-hk/cardinal-spec)).
- **Rule relaxed:** "no committee" (and "no checkpoint").
- **Privacy:** same as (d). Committee members also see swap metadata.
- **Reuse:** R2's settlement core. Signature checks are cheap on Cardano (CIP-49 / Ed25519).
- **Your PC:** trivial.
- **Maturity:** high for multisig; alpha for Partner Chains.
- **Toward trustless:**
  - *What it teaches:* exactly what statement the settlement needs (txid + height + finality), so it's a cheap way to finish R2 end-to-end on testnet.
  - *Upgrade path:* honest-majority committee → 1-of-n optimistic challengers (Cardinal model) → the permissionless relay in (d) → proofs (g).
  - *Weaker than (d)* because the trust has to be removed later instead of never being added. Good only as a 1–2 week testnet scaffold.

### (f) State of ZK verification on Cardano

- Groth16 on BLS12-381 is affordable: ~2.05–2.71B CPU of the 10B budget (§1). Cost depends on the number of public inputs, not on circuit size ([Cardano dev portal](https://developers.cardano.org/docs/developers/curriculum/smart-contracts/advanced/zero-knowledge/)).
- Halo2/PLONK verification is proof-of-concept and near or over the budget (§1). The CIP-0133 MSM builtins should lower it ([CIP-133](https://cips.cardano.org/cip/CIP-133)).
- **[analysis]** R1 and R2 were right to pick Groth16 on BLS12-381 as the *final* wrapper. The problem was what went inside it: a 652-piece STWO verifier. Keep a single Groth16 wrapper; shrink what it proves.

### (g) Zcash light-client proving costs, zkVMs, and hardware

- **Zoro** (StarkWare-Bitcoin team): a Cairo/STWO program proving Zcash header chains (Equihash, difficulty, ZIP 221 history, ZIP 244 commitments), with recursion for long ranges ([forum](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/zoro-a-stark-proven-zcash-light-client-in-cairo/53220), [repo](https://github.com/starkware-bitcoin/zoro) **[repo not opened this session]**).
  - The projected receipt is "50–200 KiB", but a reviewer found observed STWO proofs of 717 KB–8.2 MB at the time (Nov 2025).
  - **No per-header proving-time figure was found [unverified].**
  - Your R1 ran into the Cairo suite aborting at a 3.5M KiB cap (DESTINATION-CHECKPOINT.md).
- **ZULU** reportedly verifies Equihash on Starknet split across 11 transactions ([zcash-relay-on-starknet](https://github.com/scr1ptjunk13/zcash-relay-on-starknet)). This came from a search summary only **[unverified]**.
- **SP1 / RISC Zero:** Zebra's Rust Equihash verifier could run as a zkVM guest program. I found no published Equihash zkVM benchmark **[unverified]**.
- **AMD GPU:**
  - sppark has limited AMD (HIP) support, with BLS12-381 G1 MSM tested on gfx90a/gfx1100/gfx1101/gfx1201 ([sppark PR 82](https://github.com/supranational/sppark/pull/82)).
  - ICICLE officially supports NVIDIA CUDA; Metal is experimental, Vulkan in progress ([ICICLE FAQ](https://dev.ingonyama.com/start/integration-&-support/faq_and_troubleshooting)).
  - A RISC Zero fork has HIP proving tested on an RX 9070 XT, which is RDNA4 ([hemilabs commit](https://github.com/hemilabs/risc0/commit/af3dc8798eaad82eef505c0345a78be0ec9f846f)).
  - **The RX 6600M is RDNA2. I found no evidence of ZK GPU support for it [unverified], so don't plan on the GPU.**
- **CPU with ~63 GiB RAM:** R2's last failure was an out-of-memory at 6,771,428 KiB under a self-imposed 8,500,000 KiB cap (R2-QUOTIENT-COMPACT-BACKEND.md). **[analysis]** Lifting the cap to ~48 GiB would likely clear *that* allocation, but it doesn't fix 652 separate keys. Lifting it for a single Equihash Groth16 setup (dispute proof) or a Zoro run is the sensible use of the RAM.
- **Toward trustless:** this is the end-state machinery for (d)'s upgrades. *Needs:* published Zoro proving costs, or your own one-off measurement.

### (h) Intent / solver networks

- NEAR Intents supports ADA and transparent ZEC through MPC/TSS custody ([chain support](https://docs.near-intents.org/resources/chain-support)). Zwap uses solvers on top of an atomic protocol; per its posts, the solver never holds user funds ([thread](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/zwap-trustless-shielded-atomic-swaps-for-zcash-early-access/55874)).
- **[analysis]** Solvers are a *liquidity and user-experience layer*, not a trust model. Your (b)+(d) protocol could later be offered by solvers acting as the always-online counterparty, which also solves "who is online at match time".
- Solvers concentrate visibility of order flow, a privacy concern raised in the [Zwap thread](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/zwap-unlinkable-cross-chain-atomic-swaps/55104).

### (i) The Shielded Bitcoin paper (Sept 24, 2026) and Bitcoin PIPEs v2 (eprint 2026/186)

- **Shielded Bitcoin** ([PDF](https://www.allocinit.xyz/uploads/shielded-bitcoin.pdf)): a metaprotocol on Bitcoin L1 with encrypted notes, nullifiers and ZK proofs. Peg-in and peg-out are out of scope and depend on PIPEs v2. That matches your Sept 27 conclusion: no basis to resume (zeUTXO-REVISIT-CATALOGUE.md).
- **PIPEs v2** ([eprint](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/186), [bitcoindev post](https://groups.google.com/g/bitcoindev/c/iva-eCNrmuc)):
  - **What it does:** witness encryption locks a signing key so that only someone holding a valid SNARK can recover it. The resulting signature looks ordinary.
  - **Why it matters here [analysis]:** in principle this would give Zcash's shielded pool "proof-gated signatures" with no consensus change, i.e. a trustless ZEC-side covenant.
  - **Cost and setup:** ~330 TB per ciphertext today, with a stated path to ~100 GB. Setup uses a DKG committee under a 1-of-n honesty assumption.
  - **Toward trustless:** a long-horizon watch item. It needs witness-encryption ciphertexts to drop by orders of magnitude.

## 4. Ranked recommendation (by how well each leads to a trustless end state)

### #1: Optimistic Zcash header relay on Cardano with known-txid settlement (d), starting from a checkpoint, then upgraded to a proven checkpoint (g)
- **Why first:** it is the only path whose end state has no added trust beyond Zcash proof-of-work and one honest watcher. That is the same assumption R2-SETTLEMENT-DESIGN.md already accepted. It removes the proof graph that killed R2.
- **First step:** a paper-plus-emulator cost spike. It runs only after you approve a build step (it would run code on the box, not on GGLori, unless you say otherwise).
  1. Write an Aiken validator that accepts N Zcash testnet headers per transaction, checking linkage, Zcash's difficulty rule, the hash-below-target test, and timestamps, but *not* Equihash. Measure it in the Lucid emulator with real header bytes.
  2. Measure a Merkle-inclusion check of a known v5 txid.
  3. Confirm from the protocol spec how the Zcash block hash is computed.
- **Pass:**
  - 3,456 headers/day fit in batched transactions each ≤50% of the 10B CPU budget;
  - the daily fee cost is written down;
  - the txid inclusion check is ≤20% of the budget.
- **Fail:** any of these is false, or the Zcash block hash is not cheaply checkable on Cardano.
- **Cost:** $0, no funds; ~2–3 agent-days. **[analysis]** These are estimates, not measurements.
- **Step 2, if step 1 passes:** one capped measurement of a Groth16 setup and proof for R1's Equihash circuit with the memory cap lifted, budgeted at ≤4 h wall time on the 63 GiB machine. Pass if setup + prove complete and the proof verifies in the emulator under 30% of the CPU budget.

### #2: Adaptor-signature R2 with an auxiliary refund (b), deployed under a written risk limit (a) until #1 can arm it
- **Why second:** it is the user-facing protocol of the end state, and it fixes R2's main privacy leak. It's trustless under a bounded-delay assumption today.
- **First step:** a 1–2 day spec-and-data sprint with no code against funds.
  1. Write the α-aware RedPallas adaptor and Pallas↔secp256k1 DLEQ specification, citing the [Logos RFP appendix](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/logos-co/rfp/master/appendix/zcash-atomic-swap-primitives.md) and [Farcaster](https://docs.rs/farcaster_core/latest/src/farcaster_core/crypto/dleq.rs.html).
  2. Pull Zcash and Cardano block-interval and inclusion-delay data from public explorers. Draft a one-page risk limit (D, T_A, T_B) for you to sign, recalibrated for NU7.
- **Pass:** you approve a numeric D, and the specification has no unresolved step (or it is sent for an outside review).
- **Fail:** you won't accept any finite D (then only #1 remains), or the α/DLEQ construction has an open security gap.
- **Cost:** $0.

### #3: Validity-proof Zcash light client (g), as a research track feeding #1's upgrades
- **First step:** read-only research. Collect Zoro's published benchmarks and receipt sizes, and ask its maintainers for per-header proving time and RAM. Contacting them is an outward message that needs your OK.
- **Pass:** proving all headers from genesis to a checkpoint is ≤1 week of CPU on 63 GiB, and a Groth16 wrapper of the receipt verifier is plausible.
- **Fail:** costs are unpublished and the maintainers don't answer. In that case, park the track and stay on #1 with a trusted checkpoint.
- **Cost:** $0; ~0.5 day.

**Not recommended now:** committees (e), as anything beyond a short testnet scaffold; custodial networks (c); PIPEs v2 / Shielded Bitcoin (i); ZSA/TZE (c).

## 5. Answers to the audit's 13 open questions

1. **Is unbounded-delay safety a real requirement?** No design meets it, including the relay designs (§0, (d)). Replace it with "one honest watcher + a written delay bound D". Recommendation #2's first step produces that D.
2. **Bounded trust: committee, checkpoint, or owner-approved fallback?**
   - Committee: only as a scaffold (e).
   - Trusted checkpoint: yes, as a *temporary* step with a defined upgrade to a proven checkpoint ((d) → (g)). Binocular and Midnight both start from checkpoints.
   - Owner-approved fallback: keep it as an emergency exit only.
3. **Privacy needs?** Adaptor signatures (b) remove the hash link. Settlement in #1 still links the Zcash txid to the ADA escrow on Cardano. Amounts stay hidden in the note, but the fixed swap amount (R2's 1,000,000-zatoshi profile) should become variable.
4. **Is 30 minutes required, or is the two-party model required at all?** With 25 s Zcash blocks and Cardano's practical few-minute settlement, ~30 min for the happy path is plausible **[analysis]**. Relay promotion (100 blocks ≈ 42 min at 25 s, plus the window) applies only to *armed* settlement. Solvers (h) can be the counterparty.
5. **Adaptor swap with a 2-of-2 shielded note + R2 auxiliary refund?** Yes, it's the recommended variant (b). It reduces linkability but doesn't fix timing. A RedPallas adaptor construction is unpublished but described as feasible.
6. **Optimistic header relay?** Yes. Binocular is the template, with Equihash checked only in disputes via one Groth16 proof (d). Its cost is the first measurement to make.
7. **Proof system with universal setup / low memory / recursion?** Keep Groth16 as the final Cardano wrapper. Halo2/PLONK on Plutus is still proof-of-concept and near the budget. Use STWO/Zoro recursion *off-chain* and wrap it once.
8. **Existing Zcash light clients or provers?** Zoro (Cairo/STWO, draft) and a Starknet Equihash relay (ZULU, unverified). No SP1/RISC Zero Equihash benchmark found.
9. **Would hardware change the verdict?** RAM: yes, for single large setups; the 8.1 GiB cap was self-imposed. GPU: an RDNA2 RX 6600M has no evidenced ZK support. Hardware alone doesn't fix the 652-key design; architecture #1 does.
10. **How do production ZEC swaps handle trust?** All are custodial MPC/TSS vaults with transparent deposits: NEAR Intents, Maya, THORChain. The only "trustless shielded" claim (Zwap) is capped, has no external audit, and its status is unclear.
11. **Zcash consensus features coming?** NU7 (Nov 2026) adds no scripting. ZSAs are not in NU7. STARK_VERIFY TZE is a draft. Crosslink is in productionization and is the most useful future upgrade (finality certificates).
12. **Fee bumping (M09) and two-host deployment (M08 P3)?** Still needed for any route. ZIP 317 fees plus pre-signed transactions call for either fee headroom or child-pays-for-parent via the auxiliary output **[analysis; not researched further]**.
13. **Budget model?** Fix the spend per *pass/fail experiment* (the steps in §4), not per usage-meter window. That is what the audit's process finding implies.
